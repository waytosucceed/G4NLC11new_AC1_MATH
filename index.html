What is Multiplication?

Multiplication is a faster way of adding the same number multiple times.

Example:
Instead of writing 2 + 2 + 2 + 2 = 8, we write 4 × 2 = 8.

Instead of writing 3 + 3 + 3 = 9, we write 3 × 3 = 9.

Understanding Multiplication Tables:

When creating a times table, each new answer is found by adding the base number to the previous answer.

We can use the Finger Trick for table of 9:

  1. Hold your hands in front of you.
    Imagine your hands are numbered from left to right, from 1 to 10. So, your left pinky is number 1, and your right pinky is number 10.

  2. Pick the number you want to multiply by 9.
    In this case, we want to find (3 × 9).

  3. Fold down the finger that corresponds to the number you are multiplying by 9.
    For 3 times 9, fold your 3rd finger (the third one from the left).

  4. Look at the fingers to the left of the folded finger.
    These represent the tens place in your answer.
    For 3 times 9, there are 2 fingers on the left.

  5. Look at the fingers to the right of the folded finger.
    These represent the ones place in your answer.
    For 3 times 9, there are 7 fingers on the right.

  6. Combine the two numbers:
    The answer is 27.

Understanding Turnaround Facts in Multiplication:

What are Turnaround Facts?

Turnaround facts in multiplication show that changing the order of the numbers (factors) does not change the answer (product).

This means you can multiply numbers in any order, and the result will always be the same.

Example of a Turnaround Fact

Even though the numbers are swapped, the answer stays the same!

Why Does This Happen?

Think of it like arranging objects in groups. Imagine you have 3 groups of 4 apples:

Since each group has 4 apples, you count: 4 + 4 + 4 = 12 → 3 × 4 = 12

Now, if you switch it and have 4 groups of 3 apples:

Since each group has 3 apples, you count: 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 = 12 → 4 × 3 = 12

No matter how you arrange them, the total is still 12.
